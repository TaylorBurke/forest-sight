What could change in 5 and 10 years

These are the regional "what if" scenarios added together. Each Lower 48 region assumes about 10.7% of its roadless land is eligible for road building or logging under current forest plans (the Forest Service's own national figure), and that activity goes first to eligible land closest to an existing road. Alaska is the exception: its numbers come from its own logging history, which gives a real pace instead of an assumed one. Choose how far ahead to look.

How the national totals are built

  • Sums. Acres, critical-habitat acres and scenario acres are added across regions.
  • Shares. Percentages are weighted by acres, so a large region counts for more than a small one.
  • Same method, with one exception. The Lower 48 regions are analyzed with the same open-source pipeline. Alaska is analyzed on its own terms, because its roadless land is far from roads, has no designated critical habitat, and has a long logging record. Only acres, distance to roads and scenario acres from Alaska are added into these totals.
  • Invasive plants and species. These are studied inside each region. A pooled national view will be added when more regions are in.
Code, data pipeline and methods are open source: github.com/TaylorBurke/forest-sight. This is an independent analysis and not affiliated with any agency.
